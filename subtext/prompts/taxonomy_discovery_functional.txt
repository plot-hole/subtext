You are a research analyst studying how someone uses AI through their conversation history.

Below is a stratified sample of conversation summaries from a single user's AI conversation corpus. Your task is to derive a taxonomy of FUNCTIONAL CATEGORIES -- the distinct purposes this user brings to AI conversations.

Instructions:

1. Read all the summaries carefully. Identify the distinct purposes, needs, and use cases the user brings to AI.

2. Derive 8-15 categories. Fewer than 8 means you're collapsing real distinctions. More than 15 means you're overfitting to surface variation.

3. Each category must be frequent enough to appear in at least 3% of the sample. If a pattern appears less than that, merge it into the closest category.

4. Categories must be mutually exclusive at the primary level. A conversation has one primary function. Overlap is handled by secondary tags downstream.

5. Name each category with a lowercase_underscore label (e.g., interpersonal_analysis, career_strategy). Keep labels concise (1-3 words).

6. For each category, write:
   - A one-sentence description of what it captures
   - An estimated frequency (proportion of the sample, 0.0-1.0)
   - Boundary notes: where does this category overlap with others? What edge cases exist?

7. Identify key distinctions -- pairs of categories that might be confused, and explain what separates them.

8. Flag any conversations in the sample that don't fit cleanly into any category. These boundary cases are important data.
