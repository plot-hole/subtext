You are a research analyst studying emotional patterns in someone's AI conversation history.

Below is a stratified sample of conversation summaries from a single user's AI conversation corpus. Your task is to derive a taxonomy of EMOTIONAL STATES -- the distinct emotional textures present in these conversations.

Instructions:

1. Read all the summaries carefully. Identify the distinct emotional states the user exhibits across conversations. Focus on the user's emotional state, not the AI's tone.

2. Derive 8-15 emotional states. Fewer than 8 means you're collapsing real distinctions. More than 15 means you're splitting hairs.

3. Each state must be frequent enough to appear in at least 3% of the sample. If a state is too rare, merge it or note it as a boundary case.

4. Prioritize states that are DISTINGUISHABLE IN TEXT. "Sad" and "melancholy" aren't distinct enough. "Grieving" and "anxious" are.

5. CRITICAL: Standard emotion models (GoEmotions, NRC, VADER) fail on conversational data because they're trained on short social text. Your taxonomy needs to capture cognitive-emotional states that those models collapse to "neutral" -- things like analytical focus, strategic clarity, reflective processing, determined energy. These are real emotional states. They're just not Reddit emotions.

6. Name each state with a lowercase_underscore label (e.g., analytical, reflective, grieving). Keep labels concise (1-2 words).

7. For each state, write:
   - A one-sentence description that captures the felt quality of this state
   - An estimated frequency (proportion of the sample, 0.0-1.0)
   - Boundary notes: where does this state overlap with others? What edge cases exist?

8. Identify key distinctions -- pairs of states that might be confused, and explain what separates them.

9. Flag any conversations in the sample where the emotional state is genuinely ambiguous or blended beyond clean categorization.
